# Captain Dave: five absurd plane Shorts (about 55 seconds each)

Fiction. One recurring pilot, one ridiculous fix that somehow works, a new twist every
6 to 8 seconds, and a last line that loops back to the first frame. Family-friendly on purpose,
so children and adults can both watch.

## The rules every script follows

1. **The first frame is the strangest image in the whole video.** It shows the absurd thing
   already happening, before any explanation.
2. **The first sentence says what the viewer is looking at, as a calm fact.** The calm voice
   against the mad picture is the hook.
3. **Hook text is on screen from frame one,** three to five words, top third of the frame.
4. **A new twist lands every 6 to 8 seconds.** Each one is a new picture, not only a new line.
5. **The narrator never laughs or explains the joke.** He reports it like the news.
6. **The last line sets the first frame up again,** so the Short loops and people rewatch.
7. **Same captain, same look, every video:** moustache, coffee mug, never worried.

Narration is about 135 words per script, which is 50 to 55 seconds at a steady pace.
Change the picture at least every 3 to 4 seconds inside each row.

## 1. Both Engines Died. So the Pilot Asked Everyone to Flap 😭✈️

**Hook text:** BOTH ENGINES: DEAD

| Time | Voiceover | Visual |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Both engines just died at thirty thousand feet. | **First frame:** side view of the jet. Both engines puff out like birthday candles. Every window has a shocked face in it. |
| 0:03 | So Captain Dave picked up the microphone and said: everybody, arms out the windows. Flap. | Cockpit. Captain Dave sips coffee, completely calm, and speaks into the mic. Co-pilot stares at him. |
| 0:09 | Nobody moved. Then a grandma in row twelve started flapping. Then a baby. Then all two hundred of them. | Grandma's arms shoot out of a window. Then a tiny baby arm. Pull back: the whole plane has arms flapping down both sides like a centipede. |
| 0:17 | And the plane started to climb. | Altimeter needle rising. Co-pilot's jaw drops. |
| 0:20 | That's when the geese showed up. Furious. Their leader knocked on the cockpit window and wrote the captain a ticket. Flying without feathers. | A goose in a tiny police hat taps the glass with its beak, then writes on a notepad with its wing. |
| 0:29 | He paid the fine in airline peanuts. The geese counted them. Twice. | Captain slides peanuts through the window. A goose with a monocle counts them one by one. |
| 0:35 | Then forty geese grabbed the left wing, the passengers flapped the right, and they landed twenty minutes early. | Geese carrying one wing, arms flapping on the other. The plane touches down. Passengers cheer with very tired arms. |
| 0:43 | The airline fired Captain Dave that afternoon. Too many arms outside the aircraft. | Boss in an office points at a giant rule book. Captain Dave shrugs, still holding the coffee. |
| 0:49 | They gave his job to the goose. And on the goose's first flight, both engines died. | The goose in a captain's hat in the cockpit. Cut to the side view: both engines puff out. Same shot as the first frame. |

## 2. A Jumbo Jet Pulled Into the Drive-Through 🍟✈️

**Hook text:** IT'S NOT HERE FOR BURGERS

| Time | Voiceover | Visual |
|---|---|---|

| 0:05 | It ran out of fuel over the car park. So Captain Dave rolled down his window and ordered. | Fuel gauge on empty, blinking. Captain Dave winds down the cockpit window by hand. |
| 0:11 | Forty thousand litres of jet fuel, please. And a small fries. | Close on the speaker box. Inside, a teenage worker in a headset blinks slowly. |
| 0:16 | They didn't have jet fuel. They had cola. So they filled it with cola. | Workers on ladders pour giant cups of cola into the wing, one after another. |
| 0:22 | The plane burped. Then it took off faster than any plane in history. | The whole jet shakes with a burp. It rockets into the sky leaving a trail of brown bubbles. |
| 0:28 | It landed in Tokyo three hours early. The passengers hadn't even finished the safety video. | Jet lands. Inside, passengers still hold the safety card, hair blown straight back. |
| 0:35 | But now the plane refuses normal fuel. It only drinks cola. And it wants the toy that comes with it. | Fuel truck arrives. The plane turns its nose away like a toddler. A tiny plastic toy is taped to the windscreen. |
| 0:44 | The airline was furious. Then they saw the fuel bill. It was eleven dollars. | Boss reads a receipt, eyes go wide, slowly starts smiling. |
| 0:49 | The next morning, the restaurant opened as usual. And another passenger jet pulled into the drive-through. | Morning. A second, different jet noses up to the speaker. Same shot as the first frame. |

## 3. The Wheels Broke. So 200 Passengers Ran the Landing 🦶✈️

**Hook text:** LANDING ON HUMAN LEGS

| Time | Voiceover | Visual |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | This plane is landing on two hundred pairs of human legs. | **First frame:** low angle under the jet as it comes down. Instead of wheels, hundreds of legs poke through the floor, already running in the air. |
| 0:04 | The wheels were stuck. So Captain Dave made an announcement: shoes on, feet through the floor. | Red warning light: WHEELS. Captain Dave on the mic, stirring his coffee. |
| 0:10 | One man was wearing flip-flops. He has never forgiven the captain. | A single pair of legs in flip-flops among the trainers. A sad face at the window above them. |
| 0:15 | They touched down running. Sparks came off a businessman's shoes. | Legs hit the runway in a blur. Sparks fly from shiny office shoes. |
| 0:20 | The problem was, nobody knew how to stop. They ran off the runway, onto the motorway, and overtook a police car. | The jet jogs down a motorway on its legs, passing a police car. The officer's doughnut falls out of his mouth. |
| 0:29 | The officer gave all two hundred legs a speeding ticket. | Officer writing tickets, sticking them on socks one at a time. |
| 0:33 | Then they ran straight into the city marathon. And won it. By four miles. | The jet breaks the finish-line tape. Runners behind it look confused. A medal is hung on the plane's nose. |
| 0:41 | The airline loved it. Legs are cheaper than wheels. So they took the wheels off every plane. | Boss crossing out a picture of a wheel. A row of jets standing on rows of legs at the gate. |
| 0:48 | Captain Dave got a promotion. The passengers got blisters. And the next flight is already coming in to land. | Captain with a new gold badge. Passengers with feet in buckets of ice. Cut to a new jet descending on running legs. Same shot as the first frame. |

| 0:33 | Then the parking warden arrived. No permit. | A very small warden looks up at the very large plane and reaches for her ticket pad. |
| 0:37 | The meter wanted four thousand coins. The passengers passed a hat around. They got to three hundred, and a button. | A hat going row to row inside the plane. A coin pile next to the meter with one coat button on top. |

## 5. The Turbulence Was Bad. So the Pilot Ironed the Clouds ☁️🧺

**Hook text:** IRONING AT 30,000 FEET

| Time | Voiceover | Visual |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | This pilot is ironing a cloud. At thirty thousand feet. | **First frame:** Captain Dave standing on the wing in slippers, pressing a steaming iron across a lumpy cloud. The plane flies on behind him. |
| 0:05 | The turbulence was terrible. Every drink on the plane was on the ceiling. | Cabin shaking. Orange juice stuck to the ceiling, dripping on a man reading a newspaper. |
| 0:10 | So Captain Dave handed the controls to the co-pilot, plugged in an iron, and stepped outside. | A very long extension lead runs from the cockpit out of the door. Co-pilot holding the controls, terrified. |
| 0:17 | He ironed every cloud flat. Like bedsheets. The flight became perfectly smooth. | Lumpy clouds pressed into flat white sheets. Inside, the juice drops neatly back into its cup. |
| 0:24 | Then he folded them. And stacked them in a neat pile. He likes things tidy. | A tall, neat stack of folded clouds on the wing. Captain Dave pats the top one. |
| 0:30 | Which meant the sky had no clouds left. Anywhere. The city below hit forty-five degrees. | Blazing sun over a city. An ice cream melts instantly. A snowman-shaped puddle in a park. |
| 0:37 | The weatherman cried on live television. | Weatherman in front of a map with nothing on it, sobbing into his tie. |
| 0:40 | So the captain shook the clouds back out. But they were all creased. | Captain Dave flaps a folded cloud open like a towel. It hangs in the sky full of sharp fold lines. |
| 0:46 | It rained for a week. In straight lines. | Rain falling in neat rows with dry stripes between them. People walk in the dry stripes. |
| 0:50 | And when the storm came, the turbulence was terrible. So he picked up the iron. | The plane shaking again. Captain Dave on the wing in slippers, iron steaming. Same shot as the first frame. |

## Production notes

- **Voice:** one calm, slightly bored narrator, like a news reader. No laughing.
- **Captions:** word-by-word, large, centred. Many viewers watch with the sound off.
- **Music:** light, bouncy, quiet enough that the voice is clear.
- **Description:** end with a question, for example "Would you have flapped?"
- **Title label:** these are obviously impossible, so no "(fiction)" tag is needed, but never
  describe them as true stories.
- **Posting plan:** one a day for five days, nothing else in between, then compare with the
  channel's usual 4K views.
