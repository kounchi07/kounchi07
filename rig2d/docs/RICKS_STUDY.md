# Ricks Clips style study (3 shorts, 30 fps)

Study notes from three Ricks Clips shorts the user uploaded on 2026-10-27, analysed frame by frame
at 30 fps (720×1280 source). The videos and frames were kept only in the session scratchpad and
deleted afterwards; nothing from them is in the repo. Only measurements and observations are recorded here.

| ID | Title | Length |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | Why Humans Can't Pick Random Numbers | 47.2 s |
| V2 | The Window That Breaks Japan's Gambling Law | 45.1 s |
| V3 | An Unstoppable Spear vs an Unbreakable Shield | 45.1 s |

## 1. Face

| Part | What they do |
|---|---|
| Head | Near-circle with slightly flat sides, thick dark outline. Barely any blob wobble. |
| Eyes (default) | Solid dark **vertical ovals**, no highlight, about 1/9 of the head width. |
| Eyes (fear, shock, crazy) | Swap to **ring eyes**: white disc, dark ring, small dark pupil. |
| Lids | A thick flat bar laid over the top of the eyes gives the smug, half-lidded look (their most common face). |
| Brows | Short rounded bars that tilt for emotion. |
| Mouth | A filled dark shape (crescent grin, flat line, wavy "worried" line) with **white rectangular teeth**. |
| Nose and ears | None. |
| Extras | Blue sweat drops with a white highlight (1 for nervous, 4–6 for panic); a brush-stroke mustache or beard drawn on the face. |
| Hair and hats | A dark cap with glossy sheen strokes. Hat, crown, helmet, nightcap or sunglasses carry the character's identity. |
| Timing | Expressions **swap in 1 frame** on the word that motivates them. They never blend. |

## 2. Body

| Part | What they do |
|---|---|
| Torso | One **pill/bean** shape in a flat clothing color, with no neck. |
| Clothes detail | Only 2–3 flat marks (tie, pocket, badge, pen, belt). No textures. |
| Outline | Thick, about 1.3% of frame width (≈ 14 px at 1080 wide), dark and **tinted by the scene light**. |
| Rim light | A thin light line just inside the outline, on the side facing the light (torso and legs). |
| Shading | A soft darker tone on the shadow side. No hard cel-shadow shapes. |
| Arms | Thick dark sticks made of straight segments; the elbow is a sharp angle. |
| Hands | A small dark ball with no fingers that overlaps the prop it holds. |
| Legs | Long thin sticks made of **straight segments**; the knee is an angle, never a curve or bow. |
| Feet | A short L stroke. No shoes. |
| Identity | Everyone shares the same body and is told apart by hair, hat, facial hair, glasses and clothing color. |

**Takeaway:** their characters are *simpler* than ours. The polish comes from the lighting, the sets and the camera.

## 3. Character matches the scene (lighting)

Sampled face colors (the same white face in different scenes):

| Scene | Face color | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Office, day (V1 2.0 s) | `#e3d8d4` slightly lavender | Window-blind light stripes are projected across the face **and** body |
| Sunny castle (V3 3.0 s) | `#fdf5ef` near-white, warm | Long cast shadows, all in the same direction |
| Pachinko hall (V2 14.5 s) | warm | Overhead light, bloom from the machines |
| Night alley (V2 41.0 s) | `#cba9aa` dusty pink-lavender | Warm rim on the side facing the shop window |
| Candle-lit shop (V3 28.0 s) | warm orange | Candles are visible light sources |
| Forge (V3 38.5 s) | `#fde9b0` warm cream | Orange rim, warm-brown outline, blurred background |

The rules they follow every time:
1. **Tint everything:** face, clothes and the outline itself are multiplied by the scene's light color.
2. **Key + rim:** the side facing the light source is brighter and gets a warm/bright rim line; the far side falls off.
3. **Projected light:** patterns from the set (window blinds, window light pools) fall across characters.
4. **Cast shadows** of characters and props point away from the same light source.
5. **The light source is in the frame** (window, candle, forge, neon, lamp) with glow/bloom.
6. **Depth of field:** the background or foreground blurs when the camera is close.
7. **Time of day changes on the same set** to show time passing (V3 18.5–20.5 s: day → sunset → night → day in 2 seconds for "a whole week went by").

## 4. Camera and transitions

- The camera **never stops**: median frame-to-frame motion is above zero in every second of all three videos.
- Hard cuts are rare (1 per video), always on a word.
- Most scene changes are **3–5 frame cross-dissolves while the camera moves or zooms in** (V1 3.2 s, V2 1.8 s), or **continuous camera moves** (crane up from a bridge to an aerial view in V3 9–12 s; fly out of the elevator to the city in V1 24–26 s).
- A visual change happens every **1.3–2.5 s**.
- They use **magnifier inserts** for small details (V1 cheque digits) and **visual metaphors** in 3D space (giant digits rising over the city, armies drawn as colored arrows).

## 5. Script

| | V1 | V2 | V3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Words | 165 | 161 | 165 |
| Pace | 211 wpm | 215 wpm | 221 wpm |
| First word | frame 1 | frame 1 | frame 1 |
| Sentences | 12 | 11 | 11 |
| Gap between sentences | 0.3–0.6 s | 0.3–0.5 s | 0.3–0.46 s |

Story shapes:
- **V1 (claim → proof → "But how?" → analogy → mechanism → callback):** opens with a second-person claim ("If you ever fake a number…"), proves it with a story (a man who stole $2M and still got caught), asks **"But how?"** at 10 s, explains with an everyday analogy (elevator buttons) and then a second mechanism, applies both back to the man, and ends by restating the hook as a universal rule.
- **V2 (paradox → immersion → "you" → reveal → punchline):** opens with a paradox ("illegal… yet on almost every street"), shows the sensory scene, deepens the paradox (police walk past), puts the viewer in it ("you win a bucket"), follows a stranger to the secret window, pivots on **"You see,"** to the mechanism, and ends on an ironic punchline ("on paper, nobody gambles").
- **V3 (paradox setup → stakes → standoff → twist → reveal):** opens with a paradox object pair, raises the stakes (100-year war), escalates the standoff with absurd numbers, delivers a 3-word punch ("Then nobody moved."), a secret question, a short character line ("Attack and find out."), then **"You see,"** → the reveal (both were plain iron) → "that's how he ended the war."

Shared rules:
- About 160–165 words in about 45 s. The hook names the paradox or claim in the first sentence.
- **"So…"** moves the story along; **"You see,"** starts the reveal; the **last line calls back to the hook** and ends the video (no outro, no call to action).
- Mix long sentences with 2–4 word punches ("But how?", "Then nobody moved.").
- Use lists of three or more for escalation (cookies, chocolate, shampoo…).

## 6. Sound design

| Measurement | V1 | V2 | V3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Integrated loudness | −15.1 LUFS | −14.3 LUFS | −15.1 LUFS |
| Loudness range | 2.6 LU | 2.4 LU | 2.3 LU |
| Voice level (median) | −18.5 dBFS | −17.7 dBFS | −18.6 dBFS |
| Bed in pauses (median) | −30 dBFS | −25 dBFS | −32 dBFS |

- The mix is **heavily compressed and steady** (LRA ≈ 2.5 LU), mastered to about −14/−15 LUFS.
- The **music/ambience bed sits about 12–15 dB under the voice** and is always there.
- A **sound accent lands about every 1.5 s** (pops, whooshes, ticks, coins, impacts). Pauses are filled by SFX that peak close to voice level (e.g. each item in V2's prize list).
- The mix is **almost mono** (side channel −47 to −50 dBFS), which suits phone speakers.
- One narrator carries everything; short character lines are rare.

## 7. Captions

- One word at a time (2–3 for short words), shown on the beat of the word.
- White heavy rounded sans with a thick black outline and a slight shadow; no color highlight.
- Centered at about **70% of frame height**, on top of the picture.

## 8. What this means for Rig2D

1. **Scene-light matching (biggest gap):** give `Actor.render` a `light` option `{ color, dir, rim, ambient }` that tints skin, clothes and outline, adds a rim line on the lit side, darkens the far side, and lets scenes project patterns (blinds) onto characters through a clip.
2. **Simpler characters, richer sets:** our characters already have more detail than theirs. Spend effort on set density, light sources, depth of field and time-of-day variants.
3. **Face kit additions:** a lid bar ("smug"), ring eyes (already have), a wavy worried mouth, teeth blocks, and multiple sweat drops.
4. **Edit:** 3–5 frame dissolves with a push-in, continuous camera moves between sets, and a visual change every 1.5–2.5 s.
5. **Script:** 160–165 words per 45 s (~215 wpm), sentence gaps 0.3–0.45 s, a "But how?"/"You see," pivot, and a final line that calls back to the hook.
6. **Sound:** always-on bed 12–15 dB under the voice, an accent about every 1.5 s, almost mono, heavy compression, −14 LUFS.

## 9. Beat map of an opening (V1, first 10 s, re-measured 2026-10-27 at 4 fps)

Measurements only; the upload and frames were deleted after measuring.

| Time | Beat | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 s | Extreme close-up on a prop detail, then one continuous pull-back to the character | Opens on an object, never on a face; no cut |
| 1–3.5 s | Medium shot, slow push; a magnifier holds the detail on screen | Expression swaps about every 0.7 s on the motivating words (smug → grin → ring eyes + sweat) |
| 3.5 s | Dissolve (4–5 frames) with a push-in to a new location | The only soft transition |
| 3.5–5.5 s | Wide shot of a big sign whose number animates | The number carries the line |
| 5.5–6.5 s | One continuous crane down from the sign into the character | Location change by camera move, not a cut |
| 6.5–9.5 s | Medium shot; 3 expression swaps; a gag prop enters the frame | Prop arrives on the punch word |
| 9.8 s | Hard cut on the question word to a new set | The one hard cut lands on "how?" |
